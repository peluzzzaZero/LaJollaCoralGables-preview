# Cinematic art direction

The owner asked for a critical whole-site improvement rather than restoration of an earlier font. The existing title/body/thumbnail deck did not bind its content to the film: ballroom text appeared over exterior foliage, thumbnails were too small and portrait footage was enlarged on wide screens.

The reference review uses the actual Emons site and Blue World Studio's implementation case, plus Apple's iPhone 18 Pro page. Emons ties rendered video scenes to specific services; Apple demonstrates strong scale, spacing and hierarchy. La Jolla retains its own approved art and factual content. No reference assets are shipped.

Manrope is the one interface font, self-hosted as a 23 KB Latin/Spanish variable WOFF2 with its SIL licence. The owner’s typography feedback supersedes the prior serif/script retention. The ballroom chapter uses the original ballroom photo, music remains explicitly a house detail, team names/roles are direct, and chapter-specific copy replaces generic promises. Complete approved detail is retained in native disclosures.

The full-screen timeline is preserved. Desktop uses the approved panorama as a continuous forward take instead of stretching 480px portrait recordings. Phones keep the original approach and native recorded details, with a later original excerpt replacing reverse repetition. The panorama is the historical photo-conditioned shot already present, not newly generated or claimed to be a captured interior tour. Sources and treatment are documented in assets/venue/cinematic/art-direction-source.json.

Photo assembly is used only for the ballroom and brand detail. A native directory replaces the constant nine-label strip. Media remains muted, lazily delivered and poster-first, with the bounded WebKit startup and coalesced seeking intact. Visual review is required separately from CI: passing tests does not establish subjective design quality. Production DNS/GoDaddy Live and native mail configuration remain unchanged.

Desktop follow-up v1.15.1: the owner approved the mobile presentation but identified desktop problems. Six horizontal camera intervals now give each beat usable movement, with four five-second pans of unchanged original photographs. This preserves architecture and avoids the huge portrait history crop and nearly static long panorama. These photo pans are explicitly documented, not described as filmed interiors. Desktop-only short-window typography and height-aware refresh do not change phone layout or toolbar behavior.
