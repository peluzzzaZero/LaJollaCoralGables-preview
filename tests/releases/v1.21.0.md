# v1.21.0 — a visit told in scenes

The owner found the landing too simple after the entrance: one basic scroll film followed by static sections. Taking Apple's product pages and narrative studio sites as reference (sections that become scenes, images arriving as the story advances), each chapter now has its own scroll-scrubbed scene instead of the same fade:

1. Arrival — the approved approach film settles into an arched doorway at the end of its pin.
2. The house — the history paragraph and the house quote ink in word by word as they are read; the year 1928 slides into place.
3. The spaces — the staircase, music, garden and piano photographs arrive one after another as printed photographs laid on a table, inside one native sticky stage with a 01–04 counter. They keep their real proportions and captions.
4. Occasions — the row being read is marked by a rule and its numbers; on desktop the sticky image shows the current numbers.
5. The Lexington — the chapter becomes deep green and gold, matching its description; the arched mark rises into view.
6. Services and identity — service lines are written onto the page; the seven identity plates gather and settle as if spread on a table.
7. Close — the LJ mark stamps the invitation.

Mobile corrections: the cramped two-column detail grid is replaced by the stacked photographs, the identity captions no longer show a white strip, the services heading and the closing paragraph align with their headings.

No new video, generated media or invented content. The entrance film is still the only video and only GSAP pin; the album uses CSS sticky positioning in native document flow. Reduced motion, SaveData and missing JavaScript/IntersectionObserver restore the complete static layout. All copy remains in the DOM, translated in EN/ES, and the inked text is re-split after a language change.

Validation for this release: the static/content suite; Chromium responsive review (320, 390, 768, 884, 901 and 1440 px, EN/ES); entrance decoding; landing fallbacks; footer; desktop anchors; and scene checks for album forward/reverse and settled states, ink completion, scatter/seal reversal, repeated album flings, a language switch mid-album, every occasion reaching the current mark, the short-landscape grid fallback and the reduced-motion grid. WebKit decoding and the MySQL tests run in CI. The CI run, the exact-head external review (reviewer, model, effort, SHA, findings and resolutions) and the visual evidence are recorded in the pull requests, outside the source tree. Real iOS and Android devices were not available, so the WebKit arch compositing and the sticky album on older iOS Safari still need checking on actual phones. Browsers without small-viewport units or overflow clipping get the static album and no travelling lines.

Next useful improvements: owner-approved celebration photography for the occasion scene, verified venue facts, and device testing of the sticky album on older iOS Safari.
