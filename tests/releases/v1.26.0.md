# v1.26.0 — the walk through the front doors

The owner shared his own film of La Jolla's entrance (`LA Jolla.mov`, 4K, 20.5 s) and chose a scroll-drawn treatment. The stakeholders asked to keep the page's essence and only enrich the venue section.

1. "An invitation inside" — the hall photograph's frame becomes a short scroll-drawn walk: the front doors with the etched "La Jolla" glass, then the glide past reception into the hall (6.9 s of real footage, no generated or retouched pixels). The frame starts as the arch the opening film ends on, then opens as the doors part. Native sticky staging; the opening film keeps the page's only pinned scene.
2. A story told in place — "Welcome to La Jolla" rises off the doors, then three short cream notes with a sage rule appear where they belong in the room: the welcome (reception), the hall (chandeliers), and your occasion (the piano). English and Spanish. A "The front doors — The hall" track shows progress.
3. Files — 1280×720 and a 720×720 phone crop, silent, H.264 GOP 6 faststart (2.0 MB / 1.1 MB) with VP9 WebM alternatives. Provenance, cut offsets and hashes are in `assets/venue/cinematic/venue-walk-source.json`.
4. Fallbacks — reduced motion, data saving and short screens keep the original photograph and layout. The photograph also stays in the frame until the film has decoded a frame, and the section returns to its original layout if no format loads. The film is fetched only near the section, primed muted for iOS, with WebKit's native loader as a fallback, and reloads when the screen crosses the phone/desktop width.

No prices, capacity, packages or banned wording. No new generated video.

Release gate (results recorded in the feature and release pull requests): the static/content suite including `tests/venue-walk.test.mjs`, the browser review including `tests/browser/venue-walk.py` (cold load, quick scrubbing, failed-source and reduced-motion fallbacks at 320, 390, 884 and 1366 pixels in English and Spanish), an external Sonnet/high review of the exact head, and complete CI.

Limitations: story notes, the greeting and the progress steps are decorative and hidden from screen readers; the hall photograph and its caption carry the content, and visitors without the walk do not see those lines. Automated browsers run desktop engines without touch and may decode only the WebM; the MP4 path and touch priming that iPhones use are not exercised by CI and need a check on a real phone after release.

Next useful improvement: photographs of the house for the tour chapter, and the assistant the owner described, once its brief arrives.
