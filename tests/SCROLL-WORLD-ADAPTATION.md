# Scroll World + Elaya adaptation

The user requested both skills and photo-conditioned short Venice clips. Their existing La Jolla brand takes precedence over the template's miniature art and mandatory sans-serif font rules. There are nine factual chapters with one primary planning route. Camera clips, reading intervals, one word reveal and selected real-photo slices share one timeline; more detailed content remains within its native expandable chapters. Existing approved media remains intact.

The camera uses Scroll World's endpoint-preserving linger mapping, coalesced seeks, poster-first decoding, nearby loading and mobile priming. The implementation retains the existing tested ScrollTrigger pin and cleanup rather than replacing the page with an unrelated standalone template. Mobile URL-bar-only changes keep the configured scroll span.

Elaya's contribution is the short benefit-led copy, one clear planning action, meaningful line breaks, spacing/type hierarchy, floating navigation, complete interaction states and a reading reveal reserved for one occasions statement. The existing serif brand fonts, script signature, sage and cream palette are explicit retained brand choices. No fake testimonials, capacities, offers, guarantees or pricing were introduced.

Version 1.15.0 restores the original editorial navigation and brand hierarchy after owner feedback about repetitive motion. Headlines and ordinary body copy remain fully readable, photo assembly appears only in history and brand details, and chapter layouts alternate on mobile and desktop. Three chapters let the film stand without an inset photo. Mobile priming/source delivery is unchanged.

Version 1.14.0 produced three five-second Venice Seedance Mini generations quoted at 0.47 USD each, with no top-up. Version 1.14.1 removes the ballroom takes from the landing after the owner identified mobile ceiling distortion and insufficient motion. Those chapters now use five-second cuts of owner-supplied recorded facade films, cropped for mobile without generation or optical flow. The per-chapter camera intervals preserve visible movement and the existing reading/photo choreography. MP4s have no audio; media is self-hosted and no generation API is called by visitors. Both historical and corrected provenance are recorded in the cinematic asset manifests.
